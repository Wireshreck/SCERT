# Chapter 04 - Redox Reactions

Experiments and balanced equations introduce chemical change and conservation of mass: in a reaction total reactant mass equals total product mass. A chemical equation is balanced by making atom counts equal on both sides, changing coefficients rather than formula subscripts. Oxidation is electron loss and reduction electron gain; both occur together in redox reactions. Oxidising agents promote oxidation and are themselves reduced; reducing agents promote reduction and are themselves oxidised. Oxidation number tracks electron accounting: elemental substances have 0; in compounds the sum is zero, and common values (H +1, O −2, alkali metals +1, halogens −1) support calculations. Increasing oxidation number signals oxidation; decreasing signals reduction. Examples include Na/Cl, Mg/O, carbon combustion, Zn/HCl and everyday combustion, respiration and corrosion. The chapter contains worked balancing and oxidation-number examples, assessment questions and lab investigations.
