# Chapter 06 - Solutions

A solution is a homogeneous mixture of solute and solvent; examples include liquid solutions, alloys and atmospheric air. The text uses “like dissolves like” to explain why polar/ionic solutes often dissolve in polar water while non-polar solvents behave differently, and illustrates salt hydration. Concentration is the amount of solute dissolved in a fixed amount of solvent; mass percentage is solute mass divided by solution mass times 100. Solutions are classified as unsaturated, saturated and supersaturated at a given temperature. Solubility is the grams of solute needed to saturate 100 g of solvent at a given temperature; graphs compare salts and show anomalous Glauber’s salt behaviour. Temperature, solvent/solute nature and pressure (for gases) affect solubility. It distinguishes true solutions, suspensions and colloids by particle visibility, settling, filtration and light scattering (Tyndall effect), and discusses colloid types, food stabilisers and preservatives.
