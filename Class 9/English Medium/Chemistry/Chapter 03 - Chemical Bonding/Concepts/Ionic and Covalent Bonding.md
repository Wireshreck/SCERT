# Ionic and Covalent Bonding

Ionic bonding forms when atoms transfer electrons and oppositely charged ions attract, as Na⁺/Cl⁻ in NaCl and Mg²⁺/O²⁻ in MgO. Covalent bonds form through shared electron pairs: one pair is a single bond, two a double bond, three a triple bond. Lewis electron-dot diagrams mark valence electrons. The chapter associates ionic compounds with crystalline solids, relatively high melting/boiling points and conductivity when molten or aqueous; covalent compounds may be solids, liquids or gases, often have lower melting/boiling points and generally do not conduct electricity. These are textbook-level generalizations.

**Textbook reference:** Chemistry Standard IX Part I, printed pp. 45-55.
