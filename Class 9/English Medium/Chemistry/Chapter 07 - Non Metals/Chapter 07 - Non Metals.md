# Chapter 07 - Non Metals

The chapter studies hydrogen, carbon, nitrogen, oxygen and chlorine through preparation, properties, reactions and uses. Hydrogen is prepared in the lab from zinc and dilute acid and industrially by water electrolysis; its combustion yields water and its high calorific value is balanced against storage and explosion limitations. Carbon allotropes include crystalline diamond, graphite, graphene and fullerene, and amorphous charcoal/coke/lampblack/coal; structures explain hardness, electrical conductivity and uses. Carbon dioxide preparation/identification and carbon monoxide toxicity are covered alongside carbonate tests. Nitrogen fixation, atmospheric reactions and fertilizers connect nitrogen to plant growth. Oxygen preparation and uses lead to ozone formation, UV absorption, ozone depletion and CFCs. Chlorine preparation, bleaching action requiring moisture, bleaching powder and chloride-ion identification are supported by apparatus diagrams and equations.

**Textbook reference:** Chemistry Standard IX Part II, printed pp. 133-152.
