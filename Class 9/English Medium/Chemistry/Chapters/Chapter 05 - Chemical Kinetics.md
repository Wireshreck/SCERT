# Chapter 05 - Chemical Kinetics

This unit first classifies chemical changes from evidence such as colour change, gas evolution, smell, precipitate and energy change. It distinguishes combination, decomposition, double decomposition (including precipitation, gas-forming and neutralisation examples) and displacement reactions. The rate of a chemical reaction is then explored through examples (explosion, rusting, weathering and dental filling) and experiments. Factors examined are nature of reactants, concentration, surface area, temperature and catalysts. Collision theory explains that effective collisions need sufficient threshold energy; higher concentration, greater exposed solid surface, higher temperature, or a suitable catalyst can increase reaction rate. The chapter connects catalysis to Haber and Contact processes, enzymes, hydrogen peroxide decomposition and applications in nature. Assessment and extended activities include gas-volume/time graphs and controlled experiments.

**Textbook reference:** Chemistry Standard IX Part II, printed pp. 95-112.
