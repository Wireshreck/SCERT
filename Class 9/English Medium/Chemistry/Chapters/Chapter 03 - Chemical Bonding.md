# Chapter 03 - Chemical Bonding

Atoms combine through attraction between constituent particles. Noble-gas duplet/octet configurations provide a stability model. Ionic bonding involves electron transfer and electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions (NaCl and MgO diagrams); covalent bonding involves shared electron pairs (single, double and triple bonds in F₂, O₂ and N₂, then molecular examples such as H₂O and HCl). The unit compares properties of ionic and covalent compounds, introduces electronegativity and polarity, hydrogen bonding in water, valency and variable valency, and formula writing by balancing charges/valencies and simplifying indices. It applies formula construction to compounds, acids, bases and salts. Figures and tables model electron-shell transfer, Lewis dot structures, electronegativity differences, ion composition and conductivity experiments.

**Textbook reference:** Chemistry Standard IX Part I, printed pp. 43-68.
