# Chapter 02 - Periodic Table

Mendeleev arranged elements mainly by atomic mass and recurring properties; Moseley’s atomic-number evidence underpins the modern periodic law: chemical and physical properties are periodic functions of atomic number. The modern table has 7 periods and 18 groups. Main-group electron configurations help infer group and period: periods correspond to occupied shells; group 1–2 outer electrons equal the group number, and groups 13–18 add ten to the number of valence electrons. Families include alkali metals, alkaline earth metals, transition elements, boron, carbon, nitrogen and oxygen families, halogens and noble gases. The chapter distinguishes transition elements and inner-transition lanthanoids/actinoids, then examines atomic-size trends: size increases down a group as shells are added and generally decreases across a period as nuclear attraction rises without adding shells.
