# Chapter 01 - Structure of Atom

Atomic structure explains how subatomic particles determine an element and its isotope. The chapter traces evidence from discharge tubes and radioactivity to nuclear and shell models. Thomson inferred negatively charged electrons from cathode rays; Goldstein observed canal rays; Rutherford’s alpha-scattering experiment established a compact positive nucleus; Chadwick identified the neutron. Bohr described electrons in fixed energy levels. Atomic number Z counts protons (and electrons in a neutral atom); mass number A counts protons plus neutrons. Isotopes share Z but differ in neutron count and A; the text connects carbon-14 dating, carbon-13 metabolic research and deuterium heavy water to applications. Shell diagrams and tables practise configurations through atomic number 18, using K/L/M shells and the 2n² capacity rule alongside the textbook’s octet emphasis.

**Textbook reference:** Chemistry Standard IX Part I, printed pp. 7-22.
