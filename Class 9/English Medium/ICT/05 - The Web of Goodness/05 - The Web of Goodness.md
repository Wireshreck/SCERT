# Chapter 5 — The Web of Goodness

**Standard IX · English Medium · ICT · printed pp. 58–76**

This chapter presents the internet as a space for collaborative learning and communication that also requires care. It moves from Wikipedia and Kerala’s SchoolWiki to social media, online transactions, Samagra’s Learning Room, and cyber-safety. Learners practise creating a SchoolWiki page in the protected “Ezhuthukalari” training area before editing school resources.

## Main topics

- **Collaborative wikis (pp. 58–60):** Wikipedia is described as a shared encyclopedia that many people can edit and volunteers monitor. Wiki comes from the Hawaiian “WikiWiki” (“quick”); Ward Cunningham created the first wiki, WikiWikiWeb, in 1995. SchoolWiki is Kerala’s school encyclopedia built with MediaWiki.
- **SchoolWiki and email (pp. 60–66):** Learners find school details, practise wiki editing, and add text, headings, and images in Ezhuthukalari. SchoolWiki membership uses an email address; the chapter explains the username/domain parts of an email address and describes CAPTCHA as a human-verification method.
- **Social media (pp. 67–69):** The chapter groups uses into resource exchange, networking, social community, real-time communication, and e-commerce. It advises privacy controls, careful sharing, reporting abuse, avoiding misinformation and strangers, taking screen breaks, and seeking support from trusted adults after bad experiences.
- **Online transactions (pp. 69–71):** Examples include buying/selling, money transfers, online banking, and bill payment. The chapter introduces two-factor authentication: a password plus a one-time code delivered through a phone or email.
- **Learning resources (pp. 71–73):** The SAMAGRA Plus Learning Room offers subject resources, audio-visual material, assessment activities, self-assessment, and podcasts.
- **Cybercrime and precautions (pp. 73–75):** The chapter describes malware, phishing, cyberstalking, camera hacking, cryptocurrency theft, and IoT attacks. Its safety illustration emphasizes secure passwords, limiting personal information to credible services, updating systems, avoiding public Wi-Fi for financial transactions, reporting suspicious activity, and staying alert to new threats.
