# Layers and Non-destructive Composition

**Standard IX · ICT · Chapter 1 · printed pp. 9–10, 14–20**

A layer is a transparent sheet that holds part of an image. A poster can keep its background, fruit, bottle, text, and decorative shapes on separate layers. This lets the designer move, resize, recolour, duplicate, reorder, or remove one element without rebuilding the rest of the composition.

In the textbook’s GIMP workflow, the canvas begins with a background layer. After cutting out an image, paste it as a new layer and give the layer a useful name. Select the image’s layer before using Move or Scale. Duplicate a bottle layer when a second copy is needed. Layer ordering determines which item appears in front; a duplicate text layer can sit behind the original to form a shadow.

**Example:** In Figure 1.15 (p. 19), the layer panel separates the title, its shadow, two bottle copies, fruit, and the background. Editing the title’s position therefore does not reposition the fruit or background.
