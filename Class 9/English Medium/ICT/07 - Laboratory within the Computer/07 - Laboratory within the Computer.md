# Chapter 7 — Laboratory within the Computer

**Standard IX · English Medium · ICT · printed pp. 20–34**

The chapter uses educational software as a learning aid through three practical sections: constructing and exploring geometry in GeoGebra, investigating element properties with Kalzium, and modelling refraction in PhET’s Bending Light simulation. The pages include GeoGebra tool icons and construction diagrams, periodic-table and atom-model screenshots, a Kalzium element-details screen, and ray diagrams with angle measurements.

## Topics

- **GeoGebra geometry (pp. 20–24):** explore tools for points, lines, polygons, circles and angles; investigate equally spaced parallel lines, triangle side ratios, and dividing a line segment into three equal parts. Learners save constructions.
- **Kalzium and periodic-table exploration (pp. 25–31):** inspect element positions, atomic data, electron configurations and shell models; compare groups and periods; use schemes to view main groups, families, iconic representations and search results. Tables prompt learners to record element properties.
- **PhET Bending Light (pp. 32–34):** use a movable light source and material selectors to observe refraction; measure incidence and refraction angles and compare paths through air, water and other media. The book states that the ray bends toward the normal from air into water and does not change direction when both media are the same.

## Activities and assessment

Learners identify GeoGebra tools; test the proportional division shown by a parallel line in a triangle; construct a 19-unit line divided into three equal parts; build shapes; explore element details and classify families; infer element properties from shell models; and use PhET to compare incidence/refraction angles and prism paths.
