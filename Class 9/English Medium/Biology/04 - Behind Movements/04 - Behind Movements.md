# Chapter 4 — Behind Movements

## Chapter overview

The chapter begins with the diversity and purposes of movement across living organisms: obtaining food, breathing, locomotion and responses to stimuli. It treats movement as an energy-requiring process and compares microscopic movement (flagella, pseudopodia and cilia), animal movement, and plant movements despite plants not moving from place to place.

In humans, movement depends on coordinated muscles and the skeletal framework. The axial skeleton lies along the body’s central axis; the appendicular skeleton includes limbs and girdles connected to it. Bones support and protect the body; the periosteum covers bone and osteoblasts deposit minerals and support growth and repair. Cartilage is softer and more flexible, cushions joint ends, and lacks blood vessels and nerves.

The chapter compares hydroskeletons (fluid pressure in organisms such as earthworms), exoskeletons (for example chitin in insects and calcium-carbonate shells) and endoskeletons (bones and cartilage in vertebrates). Its synovial-joint diagram labels ligaments, capsule, cartilage, synovial membrane and synovial fluid. It then relates muscle types and exercise to movement and discusses disorders of bones and muscles. The closing sections connect structural similarities and differences with evolution and introduce plant movement.

## Chapter topics

Movement diversity; muscles; skeletal system; structural frameworks; joints; exercise and disorders; evolution; plant movement
