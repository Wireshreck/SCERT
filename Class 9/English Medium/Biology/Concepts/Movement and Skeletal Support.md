# Movement and Skeletal Support

Movement occurs at several scales: cell and substance movement, movement of body parts, and whole-organism locomotion. The book contrasts flagella, pseudopodia and cilia in microorganisms with movement in animals and responses in plants. In humans, the axial skeleton forms the central framework and the appendicular skeleton attaches limbs and girdles. Bones support and protect; cartilage is more flexible and reduces friction at joints.
