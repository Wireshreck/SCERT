# Sound Production and Propagation

Sound is produced by vibration. A vibrating source creates pressure variations that travel through a material medium as a longitudinal disturbance; solids, liquids and gases can transmit sound, while vacuum cannot. Speed depends on medium and temperature. A sound heard after a flash is delayed because light travels far faster than sound in air.

Hearing involves a sound source, a propagation medium and the ear. The textbook diagram identifies the pinna, auditory canal and tympanum; incoming sound makes the tympanum vibrate. Musical tones are associated with regular vibrations; noise with irregular, unwanted sound. The chapter links high noise exposure with harm and recommends ear protection and community noise reduction.
