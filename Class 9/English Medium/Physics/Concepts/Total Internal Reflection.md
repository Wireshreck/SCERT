# Total Internal Reflection

When light travels from an optically denser medium toward a rarer medium, the refracted angle increases with the incident angle. At the critical angle, refraction is along the boundary (90° to the normal). Above the critical angle, the ray reflects entirely back into the denser medium: total internal reflection. It requires travel from denser to rarer medium and incidence greater than the critical angle. The textbook gives 42° for glass–air.

The diagrams connect repeated internal reflection with optical fibres. Applications shown include fibre communication/endoscopy, prism periscopes and small-prism vehicle reflectors. Atmospheric refraction and temperature-dependent air layers explain mirage and apparent positions of celestial objects; these atmospheric examples involve continuously varying conditions and are distinguished from reflection inside a bounded optical fibre.
