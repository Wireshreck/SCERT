# Momentum and Impulse

Momentum `p=mv` is a vector with the direction of velocity. Its SI unit is kg·m/s. Greater mass or velocity gives greater momentum. Newton’s second law links applied resultant force to the rate of momentum change. Impulse is force multiplied by the time interval; for a constant force, impulse equals momentum change.

The book’s catching examples show why lengthening stopping time reduces the force for the same change in momentum: hands move backward, and padding or a foam bed extends collision time. A large force acting briefly (for example, a bat striking a ball) is called impulsive force. See [[../Formulas/Force Momentum and Impulse]].
