# Chapter 1 — Refraction of Light

## Chapter overview

Light travels in a straight line within one uniform medium. At an oblique boundary between media where its speed differs, its direction changes: refraction. The bent-straw, apparent raised coin and trough activities trace the effect to the changed speed at the boundary. Optical density describes how a medium affects light speed; the chapter distinguishes it from ordinary material density. The ray diagrams show a ray bending toward the normal on entering an optically denser medium, away from it when entering a rarer medium, and not bending at normal incidence. Incident ray, refracted ray and normal lie in one plane.

The chapter defines refractive index relative to vacuum, uses n=c/v, and compares media using tables and numerical exercises. It then develops the critical angle and total internal reflection for light travelling from denser to rarer media. The glass–air critical angle is given as 42° in this edition. Atmospheric refraction explains star twinkling, apparent solar position and mirage. Total internal reflection is applied in reflectors, prism periscopes, optical fibres and endoscopy.
