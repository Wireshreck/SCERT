# Refraction and Optical Density

Refraction is the change in direction of light as it crosses obliquely between media with different optical densities because its speed changes. Light continues straight in a single uniform medium; at normal incidence it does not change direction. At an oblique boundary, the ray bends toward the normal on entering the optically denser medium and away from the normal on entering the rarer medium. The incident ray, refracted ray and normal lie in one plane.

Optical density concerns light propagation and is not the same as mass density. The textbook table gives approximate speeds: air `3×10⁸ m/s`, water `2.25×10⁸ m/s`, glass `2×10⁸ m/s`, diamond `1.25×10⁸ m/s`. The lower speed indicates greater optical density and refractive index relative to vacuum. A straw appearing bent, a coin becoming visible after water is poured, and an apparently raised pond bottom are examples of changed ray paths.
