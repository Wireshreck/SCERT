# Chapter 7 — Electric Current

## Chapter overview

The chapter uses water height, heat and air pressure as analogies for potential differences that sustain flow. Electric current is charge flowing per unit time, `I=Q/t`, measured in amperes. Potential difference is work per unit charge, `V=W/Q`, measured in volts; cells provide emf. Current direction is defined from positive to negative, opposite electron drift in a metal conductor. Circuit symbols, cells, LEDs, ammeters and voltmeters are introduced. An ammeter is in series; a voltmeter is connected in parallel across a device.

Cell combinations are compared: ideal identical cells in series add emf, while parallel combinations sustain supply longer at the single-cell emf. Ohm’s law relates current and voltage at constant temperature: `V=IR`; resistance opposes current and is measured in ohms. Experiments vary conductor material, length, thickness and temperature; longer conductors have greater resistance, a larger cross-section less, and materials differ. Rheostats vary effective length. Series resistances add; parallel branches share voltage and lower equivalent resistance. The chapter warns against connecting classroom experiments to household mains.
