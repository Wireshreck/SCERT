# Electric Current and Circuits

Current is the rate of charge flow through a conductor. A source maintains potential difference, which is work done per unit charge. Conventional current direction is positive-to-negative; electron motion in a metal is opposite. A closed conducting path with source and components forms a circuit. Ammeters are connected in series to measure current; voltmeters are placed in parallel across a component to measure potential difference.

Ohm’s law: at constant temperature, potential difference across a conductor is proportional to current; `V=IR`. Resistance depends on material, length, cross-sectional area and temperature. In series, current is common and equivalent resistance adds. In parallel, potential difference is common and branch currents add; equivalent resistance is less than the individual branches. The book’s circuit activities emphasize safe low-voltage cells and explicitly caution against household mains.
