# Buoyancy and Archimedes’ Principle

A fluid (liquid or gas) exerts an upward buoyant force on an object wholly or partly immersed in it. In the chapter’s spring-balance experiment, buoyant force equals the object’s loss of apparent weight in the fluid: `W_air − W_fluid`. For a fully immersed object, the book identifies fluid density and object volume (equivalently displaced-fluid volume) as factors affecting buoyancy.

Archimedes’ principle: the buoyant force on an immersed object equals the weight of fluid displaced. For a floating object, the principle of floatation says object weight equals displaced-fluid weight. This explains how a shaped hull can displace enough water to support a heavy ship. [[../../04 - Gravitation/Concepts/Universal Gravitation]] describes the downward weight that buoyancy acts against.
