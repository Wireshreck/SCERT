# Distance, Displacement, Speed and Velocity

Distance is the total length of the path travelled; displacement is the straight-line directed change from starting position to final position. Distance is scalar and displacement is vector. Displacement magnitude cannot exceed distance and is zero when an object returns to its start. They are equal only for straight motion in one direction.

Average speed is distance divided by elapsed time. Average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time and its direction is the displacement direction. Thus equal travel distances can produce different displacement and velocity when paths differ. Circular motion is a useful counterexample: speed may stay constant while velocity changes because direction changes.
