# Chapter 2 — Equations of Motion

## Chapter overview

The chapter begins with paths between positions to distinguish distance, the length of the route, from displacement, the directed straight-line change between initial and final positions. Distance is scalar; displacement is vector. Their magnitudes coincide only for straight travel in one direction. Speed uses distance per time, while velocity uses displacement per time and has direction. Uniform velocity requires both magnitude and direction to remain constant; circular motion can have constant speed but changing velocity.

Acceleration is change of velocity per time; a decrease in velocity is treated as negative acceleration/retardation. Tables and examples introduce uniform and non-uniform acceleration, position–time and velocity–time graphs, graph scales, slopes and area under a velocity–time graph. For uniform acceleration the chapter derives and applies `v=u+at`, `s=ut+½at²`, and `v²=u²+2as`. Worked examples include a train crossing a bridge, travel graphs, braking, vertical motion and unit conversion. Road signs, crossings, markings and pedestrian safety provide applications.
