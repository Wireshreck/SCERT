# Chapter 8 — Sound

## Chapter overview

Sound is a form of energy that produces hearing. A vibrating object produces sound; tuning-fork, rubber-band and instrument activities identify vibrating parts, including strings, reeds, membranes, vocal cords, wings and air columns. Sound requires a material medium and travels through gases, liquids and solids but not vacuum. A bell-jar evacuation demonstration illustrates this. Sound travels at different speeds in different media; the textbook gives approximate values at 25°C and notes temperature dependence. Light reaches the eye much sooner than sound reaches the ear, explaining thunder/lightning delay.

The ear diagrams trace sound vibrations through the outer ear and tympanum. Hearing requires a source, a medium and a hearing organ. The closing section addresses hearing impairment, inclusion and hearing aids, ear protection, music versus noise, and noise pollution and mitigation.
