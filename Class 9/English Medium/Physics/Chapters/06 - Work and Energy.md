# Chapter 6 — Work and Energy

## Chapter overview

In the textbook’s physics sense, a force does work when it causes displacement in the direction of that force. Work is `W=Fs` for constant force parallel to displacement; opposite force gives negative work, and no displacement means no work by that force. Work and energy share the joule as SI unit. The chapter develops potential energy as stored by position/configuration and kinetic energy as energy of motion. It derives `Eₚ=mgh` and `Eₖ=½mv²`, then uses falling objects and a pile driver to illustrate conversion and conservation of mechanical energy.

Energy is the ability to do work. Examples trace transformations through a balloon toy car and hydroelectric power station: stored water’s potential energy becomes kinetic, mechanical and electrical energy. The conservation principle states that energy is transferred or transformed without being created or destroyed in the described system. Power is rate of doing work, `P=W/t`; units include watt, kilowatt, megawatt and horsepower.
