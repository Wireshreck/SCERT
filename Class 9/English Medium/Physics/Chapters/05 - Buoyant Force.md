# Chapter 5 — Buoyant Force

## Chapter overview

Objects immersed in liquids and gases experience an upward force from the fluid, called buoyant force. The chapter measures it through loss of apparent weight: weight in air minus weight in fluid. Comparison experiments vary fluid density and the immersed object’s volume. Buoyancy increases with fluid density and displaced volume. A ship floats because its overall shape displaces enough water; a submerged iron nail does not displace enough fluid relative to its weight.

The overflow-can activity leads to Archimedes’ principle: buoyant force equals the weight of fluid displaced by an object fully or partially immersed. The principle of floatation states that a floating object’s weight equals the weight of displaced fluid. The chapter then defines relative density as a dimensionless ratio to water density and illustrates measurement with hydrometers and lactometers.
