# Chapter 3 — Laws of Motion

## Chapter overview

Force is a vector and multiple forces combine to produce a resultant. Balanced forces have zero resultant and do not change rest or uniform motion; unbalanced external force changes the state or direction of motion. Activities with carts, pulleys and Galileo’s inclined plane lead to Newton’s first law and inertia. Examples distinguish inertia of rest and motion and relate greater mass to greater inertia.

Momentum is mass times velocity and has the velocity’s direction. The chapter compares momentum and its rate of change, then develops Newton’s second law and `F=ma`. Short-duration large forces are impulsive; impulse `FΔt` equals change in momentum, explaining follow-through, catching hands moving backward and cushioning. Newton’s third law states equal and opposite forces act simultaneously on different bodies; balloon, spring-balance, rowing, rocket and jumping examples show action–reaction pairs.
