# Free Fall, Mass and Weight

Acceleration due to gravity `g` is the acceleration caused by gravitational attraction. At Earth’s surface the book uses approximately `9.8 m/s²`; on the Moon it gives `1.62 m/s²`. From universal gravitation, `g=GM/R²`, so the acceleration for a given celestial body does not depend on the falling object’s mass. Air resistance can make a feather and coin fall differently; in an evacuated tube they fall together.

Mass measures matter and remains the same when an object is moved between planets. Weight is gravitational force, `W=mg`, and changes with local `g`; the chapter contrasts common and spring balances and explains apparent weightlessness in free fall. Circular motion requires inward centripetal force; gravity supplies this for an orbiting satellite. The section distinguishes circular speed (magnitude) from velocity (which continuously changes direction).
