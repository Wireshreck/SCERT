# Chapter 4 — Gravitation

## Chapter overview

Gravity is Earth’s attraction toward its centre; the opening illustrations connect falling objects, opposite sides of Earth and lunar exploration. Experiments with spring balances compare attraction as object mass and distance from Earth change. Newton’s universal law states that every mass attracts every other mass: force is proportional to the product of masses and inversely proportional to the square of their separation. The text gives `G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg²` and worked examples for mutual attraction.

The chapter derives acceleration due to gravity from universal gravitation and Newton’s second law (`g=GM/R²`), then compares Earth and Moon values and discusses free fall, air resistance, feather-and-coin demonstration, mass versus weight, kilogram-weight, weightlessness and circular motion. A circular path requires centripetal force; gravity supplies it for orbiting satellites. Assessment questions apply these ideas to falling objects, planets, the Moon and satellites.
